Overview of PG grinding

PG grinding is commonly used as shorthand for profile grinding in Japanese and Asian precision-tooling supply chains. The process creates controlled two-dimensional contours, formed cutting edges, steps, radii and reliefs in hard materials by grinding the required profile rather than only producing a flat surface. It is relevant to connector punches, precision stamping tools, carbide inserts, micro blades and mold wear components where the profile itself is a functional feature.

Jinyi Precision lists WAIDA PGX-2500SP profile-grinding equipment for this work. A PG quotation is based on the complete contour, material condition, grinding access, edge geometry, thickness, datum surfaces and inspection method. The process may be combined with wire EDM, surface grinding, sinker EDM or blank preparation. PG grinding should therefore be considered as one controlled stage in a route, not as a universal method for every feature on the part.

Application Industries

Connector and electronic-component tooling frequently contains small formed punches and blades whose working contour affects terminal shape, burr formation and fit. Precision stamping applications may require punch, die or insert profiles that remain consistent across replacement components. Mold manufacturers use profile grinding for inserts, wear details and formed edges when the required shape and material are better suited to grinding than to conventional milling.

PG grinding is also considered for gauges, special cutting tools and automation wear components when a profile must be compared optically to drawing geometry. Prototype orders focus on proving manufacturability and profile interpretation. Repeat orders require controlled revisions, consistent datum setup and agreement on how the optical outline is judged. The buyer should state whether the component must interchange with an existing tool or match a previously approved sample.

Materials

Profile grinding is commonly reviewed for tungsten carbide, tool steel and hardened steel. Each material responds differently to wheel selection, stock removal, edge support and the risk of chipping or heat-related damage. For carbide, the exact grade and binder system matter; for steel, the heat-treatment state and hardness should be stated. Jinyi’s website lists Japanese carbide series and permitted alternatives, but the final grade must be confirmed on the customer drawing or material specification.

When substitution is allowed, the RFQ should describe the working contact, impact level, wear mode, expected service condition and coating requirement. A broad description such as “equivalent carbide” is not enough to guarantee equivalent behavior. If the buyer supplies a blank, identify existing datum surfaces, remaining stock and any area that must not be reground. Coating thickness and whether dimensions are accepted before or after coating should be agreed before the process route is fixed.

Tolerance and Inspection

The published PG references are a minimum radius of R0.03, tolerance around ±0.002 mm for suitable geometry and Ra0.05–Ra0.15 for applicable ground surfaces. These are screening references taken from the existing equipment information, not guarantees for every dimension. Profile length, material, thickness, edge angle, unsupported sections, wheel access, datum quality and the measurement method can change what is achievable.

Optical projection can compare a formed contour with drawing geometry, but the acceptance method still needs definition. Buyer and supplier should agree the magnification, contour orientation, datum alignment and whether the comparison uses CAD data, a plotted profile or dimensioned features. Thickness, parallelism, height and positional relationships may require additional instruments. A profile can appear correct while its relationship to an assembly datum is wrong, so the drawing should separate contour tolerance from location tolerance.

Equipment and Process Route

Jinyi lists six WAIDA PGX-2500SP profile-grinding machines and TALIANG CNC plotting equipment, supported by optical projection, height-gauge and microscope inspection. Equipment quantity alone does not define capability. The workpiece must be located from stable reference surfaces, sufficient grinding stock must remain, and the profile must be accessible to the selected wheel and dressing method.

A typical sequence may include blank preparation, heat treatment where required, datum surface grinding, rough profile creation, PG finishing and optical inspection. Wire EDM may remove bulk stock or prepare a contour before final grinding. The engineer reviews where to leave stock, how to support thin sections and which surface controls the profile position. For coated parts, the route must also define whether the edge is finished before coating and how coating allowance affects final acceptance.

Profile Drawing and RFQ Requirements

A useful PG drawing shows the complete working profile, basic dimensions, radii, angles, reliefs, cutting-edge condition and the datums used to locate that profile. Provide a dimensioned PDF and include DXF, DWG, STEP or STP data where it helps define the contour. State material grade, hardness, blank condition, quantity, coating, required surface roughness and delivery target.

Mark the functional edge and any area where a radius, chamfer or edge break is prohibited. If the part mates with another punch, die or connector feature, provide the relevant assembly relationship. For repeat components, include the approved revision and identify any limit sample or inspection overlay. Jinyi can sign an NDA for confidential drawings; files can be sent by email or WhatsApp after the RFQ form is submitted.

Quality Control

Quality control for a PG component should cover more than the visible outline. The plan may include profile comparison, working-edge condition, thickness, parallelism, distance from datum surfaces and surface roughness where specified. Sharp edges require careful handling during cleaning, inspection and packing because a dimensionally correct component can still be unacceptable if the working edge is damaged.

Jinyi maintains a valid ISO certification, with its exact certification name, scope and validity controlled by the current certificate. Project-specific documentation should be agreed during quotation. If the buyer needs a dimensional report, profile overlay, material certificate, coating record or photographs of protected packaging, those deliverables must be identified before production so the applicable inspection and document controls can be planned.

Sampling and Repeat-Order Control

The first sample should establish the agreed profile interpretation, datum setup and measurement method, not only a list of isolated dimensions. If the sample is approved, keep the drawing revision and inspection basis linked to the part number. Replacement punches or inserts may need to remain interchangeable with tooling already in production, so changes in stock condition, material grade or coating must be reviewed.

For overseas orders, edge protection and separated packaging should be defined before shipment. Technical questions can be resolved by email or WhatsApp, but the final decision should be recorded in the drawing, quotation or inspection agreement. This creates a consistent reference for purchasing, engineering, production and incoming quality teams.

Project Review Example: Connector Punch Profile

For a tungsten carbide connector punch with a fine formed edge, the review would identify the critical contour, material grade, blank condition, edge support, allowed radius and the datum that locates the profile. PG grinding and optical projection may be selected for the contour, while other surfaces can require surface grinding or wire EDM. The inspection plan would define how the specified ±0.002 mm feature is interpreted and measured.

This example explains a review method and does not claim a customer result, tool life or production approval. Actual acceptance depends on the approved drawing, agreed measurement method and project inspection record. Any customer name, sample result or performance improvement should only be published after documentary evidence and permission are available.

FAQ

Questions About PG Profile Grinding for High-Precision Mold Components

What is PG grinding used for?

PG grinding is used for formed profiles, micro blade features, carbide punches and precision inserts where shape accuracy is more important than general stock removal.

Can you grind tungsten carbide?

Yes. Tungsten carbide is one of the main material groups for PG grinding projects.

What tolerance can PG grinding achieve?

Tolerance depends on size, material and profile complexity. Jinyi lists ±0.002mm as a reference capability for suitable parts.

Can you inspect the profile?

Yes. Optical projection, height measurement and other inspection methods can be selected according to the drawing.
