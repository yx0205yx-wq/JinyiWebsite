Overview of tungsten carbide parts

Tungsten carbide parts are selected for tooling and wear applications that need high hardness, resistance to abrasive contact and controlled geometry over repeated cycles. The material is a cemented composite rather than a single universal grade, so hardness, toughness, grain size and binder content must be considered together. Jinyi Precision reviews custom carbide punches, inserts, blades, wear components and formed tooling parts from the customer drawing.

Carbide’s wear resistance does not remove design risk. Thin unsupported sections, abrupt transitions, impact loading, press fits and sharp corners can concentrate stress or increase chipping risk. A useful supplier review therefore covers the complete application, not only the final dimensions. The machining route may combine PG profile grinding, wire EDM, surface grinding and sinker EDM, with each process assigned to the features it can control reliably.

Typical Components and Applications

Connector and precision-stamping manufacturers use carbide for small punches, formed edges, cutting inserts and replaceable wear parts. Mold builders may specify carbide for inserts, guide features or areas exposed to repetitive abrasion. Automation-equipment projects can use carbide for locating, guiding or contact components where a steel part would wear too quickly, provided the assembly supports the carbide and avoids damaging impact.

Application information is essential because a grade suitable for sliding wear may not be ideal for repeated impact or bending. Buyers should describe the mating material, contact direction, lubrication, expected cycle condition, current failure mode and whether the carbide part is supported by a steel holder. For replacement tooling, include the existing assembly relationship and any history of chipping, wear or dimensional drift.

Materials

The current material list includes KD10, KD20, ME40, EF05, EF10, EF20, FD15, FD25, RD30 and RD50 series, plus alternatives from Taiwan or China when the buyer permits them. These codes must be matched to the specified manufacturer and datasheet before an equivalence claim is made. Listed grades are not necessarily interchangeable. Material properties must be confirmed against the specified manufacturer’s datasheet.

If the drawing fixes a grade, include the full designation and supplier requirement. If substitution is allowed, state required hardness or toughness balance, contact material, impact condition, service objective and cost target. Coating-ready blanks require additional discussion because coating type, thickness, masking and process temperature can affect edge condition and final dimensions. The coating provider and available documentation are confirmed for each order.

Tolerance and Inspection

Reference values on the existing equipment list include approximately ±0.002 mm for suitable PG-ground features and up to ±0.001 mm for applicable oil-wire EDM features. They do not apply automatically to a complete carbide part. Grade, thickness, profile length, edge geometry, grinding access, unsupported sections, surface requirement and inspection method all affect the feature-level tolerance that can be confirmed.

Carbide inspection may include optical profile comparison, thickness and height measurement, hole or slot checks, edge review under magnification and surface-roughness measurement where specified. A drawing should identify critical working edges, press-fit diameters, assembly datums and surfaces that must remain free of chips. The inspection method and whether a dimension is accepted before or after coating should be agreed before production.

Equipment and Process Route

Available process equipment listed for carbide work includes WAIDA PG profile grinding, Seibu Electric wire EDM, Sodick AD30LS sinker or mirror EDM, and precision grinding and inspection equipment. PG grinding is suited to formed profiles and working edges; wire EDM can create conductive contours and slots; sinker EDM can address suitable features that a travelling wire cannot reach; surface grinding controls reference faces and thickness.

The route starts by identifying stable datums and deciding which surfaces are finished first. Stock allowance, wire-entry strategy, edge support, electrode access and cleaning must be reviewed before machining. A component may move through several operations, but every extra setup can influence datum transfer. The quotation should therefore identify the planned sequence and the inspection point used to confirm the relationship between ground, wire-cut and EDM features.

Design and RFQ Requirements

Provide a controlled PDF drawing and a STEP or STP model when available. Identify the exact carbide grade or performance requirement, quantity, working environment, mating material, coating, critical dimensions, surface finish, edge condition and target delivery. If the part fits a holder or existing tool, include the mating dimensions and assembly method. Mark any thin section or sharp edge that must not be modified.

For a replacement component, state the previous failure mode and whether the objective is wear reduction, improved edge stability, interchangeability or another measurable requirement. This does not guarantee a specific service-life improvement, but it gives the engineering review a factual basis. Contact us to discuss an NDA. Quality Control

Quality planning for carbide must include handling as well as measurement. Small profiles and sharp edges can be damaged during cleaning, optical inspection, coating or transport. The drawing should define permitted edge breaks, and the packaging plan should keep parts separated and protect functional surfaces. Visual acceptance for chips or cracks can be added when relevant to the application.

Our ISO 9001:2015 certification scope is the production of precision mold accessories. Buyers should request the current certificate when it is part of supplier approval. Material certificates, inspection reports, coating records and special packaging photographs are project deliverables only when agreed during quotation and supported by the responsible supplier.

Total Tooling Value and Repeat Supply

Carbide often costs more than common tool steel, so purchasing decisions should compare total application value rather than only unit price. Relevant factors include expected wear mode, downtime for replacement, assembly risk, coating, regrinding feasibility and the cost of a failed tool. A supplier should not promise a life multiplier without a controlled comparison, but it can help identify the information needed for a meaningful trial.

For repeat supply, keep the approved drawing, grade, coating, datum interpretation and inspection method linked to the part number and revision. A change in carbide supplier or grade should be disclosed and reviewed. Overseas communication can be handled by email and WhatsApp, while final technical decisions remain in controlled documents so purchasing and quality teams receive the same requirement.
