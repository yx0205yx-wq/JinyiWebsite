What is Wire EDM?

A practical guide for purchasing managers, mechanical engineers and quality engineers evaluating wire EDM for hard materials, complex profiles and precision tooling.

How Wire EDM Removes Material

Wire electrical discharge machining removes material by controlled electrical discharges between a moving wire electrode and an electrically conductive workpiece. The machine follows a programmed path while controlling the discharge conditions, wire tension and flushing. Unlike milling, it does not use a rotating cutter in contact with the part.

The process is useful for profiles, slots and tooling features in hardened steel and tungsten carbide. The choice depends on the feature: a through-contour that the wire can reach presents a different problem from a blind pocket. Start with the required geometry, material condition and inspection datums rather than choosing a process solely from an advertised tolerance.

Applications, Wire Access and Internal Corners

Typical applications include precision mold inserts, connector tooling, punches and plate components with detailed contours. Cutting after heat treatment can avoid having to finish a hardened profile with a small milling cutter. Thin or delicate features still need adequate support, even though wire EDM avoids conventional tool-cutting forces.

Closed internal contours need a suitable starting hole and a continuous wire path. Internal corner radius is limited by wire diameter and EDM gap. A sharp corner on a drawing should therefore be reviewed as a defined radius or a permitted relief. Neither the process name nor a fine wire alone establishes that every corner or feature is feasible.

Wire Diameter, Flushing and Finishing Cuts

Wire diameter, material thickness, contour length, flushing access and the number of skim cuts affect the route. A smaller wire can help with a tighter internal radius, but may require different cutting conditions and closer attention to stability. Thick sections and narrow features can make flushing more difficult.

Oil-dielectric and water-dielectric machines are selected for the component and surface requirements. Jinyi Precision lists Seibu Electric water-dielectric and oil-dielectric wire EDM equipment; the reference machining tolerance for its M25LP oil wire EDM process is ±0.001 mm. This is separate from a specified surface roughness and from the capability of a different wire EDM machine. The actual feature and acceptance method should be reviewed against the drawing.

Wire EDM, Sinker EDM and Mirror Finishing

A wire cannot create every enclosed feature. Sinker EDM uses a shaped electrode and may be considered for a cavity or recessed detail that is inaccessible to a through-wire path. Mirror-finish EDM describes a surface-finishing capability within EDM, not a universal finish for every cavity or material.

Jinyi Precision’s Sodick AD30LS surface roughness references distinguish steel at Ra0.4 µm from tungsten carbide at Ra0.13 µm. Identify the relevant material and surfaces when discussing these references. PG precision grinding is reviewed separately for the surface finish specified on the drawing.

For a part with both a cavity and a formed edge, the machining sequence may include more than one process. Review PG profile grinding for the ground contour, and the existing EDM equipment and machining capabilities for a broader process discussion.

Inspection and Surface Requirements

Define how the contour is located relative to the assembly datums. An optical comparator or profile projector can help inspect an outline; other dimensions may need height measurement, microscopy or coordinate measurement. The instrument should match the feature and its acceptance criteria.

Specify surface roughness, edge breaks and any recast-layer requirements where they affect fit, wear or later processing. Also identify surfaces left with grinding stock. These details prevent two quotations from covering different finishing scopes even when they use the same wire EDM process.

What Changes the Quotation

Cutting time is influenced by the total path, workpiece thickness, wire selection and finishing passes. Setup and inspection can also be significant on a small part with several related features. Mark the functional dimensions separately from general tolerances so the quotation reflects the precision actually needed.

For repeat tooling, include the drawing revision and any interchangeability requirement. If the component will be ground, coated or assembled after cutting, identify that sequence and the condition in which it must be accepted. Compare the proposed route and inspection scope as well as the unit price.
