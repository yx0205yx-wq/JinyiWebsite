Ceramic Parts for Precision Manufacturing

A practical guide for purchasing managers, mechanical engineers and quality engineers evaluating ceramic parts for precision manufacturing applications.

Select Ceramic Components for a Defined Requirement

Precision ceramic components are selected to meet application-specific requirements such as wear resistance, electrical insulation or thermal performance. These characteristics depend on the material and grade. They should be checked against the component specification rather than assumed for all ceramics.

Jinyi Precision’s existing material information lists ZKA-901, NPZ-28 and NPZ-1. These names do not establish composition, electrical properties or thermal conductivity. Provide the exact material requirement and the properties needed for your application.

Locating, Contact and Wear Applications

A ceramic pin, guide or locating component may be considered where a metal part does not meet a defined contact, wear or electrical requirement. Specify what the part must do, the mating material and the operating conditions. Where electrical insulation is required, identify the relevant material properties for review.

Material selection should follow the application. Do not assume that every ceramic offers the same thermal behavior, strength or response to impact. If alternatives may be considered, state which requirements are fixed and what evidence is needed to assess a substitution.

Contact Faces, Edge Condition and Chipping

Distinguish contact faces, clearance surfaces and edges exposed during handling. Identify which faces locate the part and whether an edge break is permitted. A general instruction such as “no chipping” is easier to apply consistently when the affected edges and acceptance method are defined.

Thin walls, unsupported edges and abrupt changes in section require a material-specific review. Chamfers or radii may be considered where the design permits them, but should not be added without agreement. The aim is to protect the required geometry while keeping assembly and handling conditions clear.

Machining and Surface Requirements

Machining and inspection must be reviewed for the actual ceramic and feature geometry. Do not choose wire EDM solely because it works on a similarly shaped metal component. A material name alone does not establish electrical conductivity or suitability for a particular process.

No universal ceramic tolerance or surface roughness follows from Jinyi Precision’s PG grinding references. Put the required dimensions and finish on the drawing for review. Identify whether the critical characteristic is a pin diameter, straightness, a locating face, flatness or a hole relationship. This avoids assigning one general accuracy claim to different ceramic components.

Inspection and Assembly Planning

Agree the datum orientation and inspection method before acceptance. Optical comparison, microscopy, height measurement or coordinate measurement may address different features; the method should fit the drawing and material. Include visual acceptance requirements where chips or cracks affect function.

Explain whether the component will be pressed, bonded, clamped or installed with clearance. The metal holder and ceramic component may respond differently to load and temperature, so identify the mating conditions rather than treating the ceramic part in isolation. For interfaces with a mold insert, the datum and assembly planning guide helps frame the dimensional discussion.

Handling and Quotation Information

Packaging should protect the specified edges and contact surfaces. Small pins may need separation, and plates may need protection from part-to-part contact. State cleaning requirements if contamination affects the process. The appropriate handling method depends on the material, geometry and surface condition.

A useful drawing package includes the exact material requirement, mating information, quantity, critical dimensions, finish and acceptance criteria. If the material has not been selected, describe the required function without assuming a particular composition. This gives the review a basis for discussing manufacturability and inspection without claiming an unverified ceramic capability.
